Let me just say it: dating after fifty is a different animal. And nobody adequately prepares you for it.

When I was in my twenties, dating was confusing but at least it came with a shared rulebook. You dated, you called it something, you maybe got serious, you figured it out. Dating at fifty has no rulebook. Or rather, it has several competing rulebooks, none of which quite fit.

## The apps are a tool, not a *solution*

Online dating apps are where most people over fifty meet potential partners now, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But the apps are a tool, and like any tool, they work better when you understand what they are for and what they are not.

They are for initial access to people you would not otherwise meet. They are not for building connection — that still happens the old-fashioned way, through conversation, through time, through showing up.

Be specific in your profile about who you are and what you are looking for. Vague profiles attract vague people. Intentional profiles attract intentional people. And at fifty, you have neither the energy nor the interest for vague.

## The baggage *conversation*

Everybody at fifty has a history. You have one. Any person worth dating has one. The question is not whether baggage exists — it is whether it is packed or whether it is still sitting open on the floor.

> What to look for: someone who can talk about their past relationships with honesty and some degree of peace. Not someone who has no feelings about the past, but someone who has processed enough to not be governed by it.

## What is actually different *this time*

Here is the good news about dating at fifty that does not get said enough: you know yourself better than you ever have. You know what you like. You know what you will not tolerate. You know what loneliness actually feels like and you know that being alone is not the worst possible outcome — being in the wrong relationship is.

That knowledge is power. It is the thing twenty-five-year-old you did not have. You no longer need to contort yourself into what someone else wants. You no longer have to pretend you are fine with things that are not fine. You can ask for what you want and walk away from what does not match it.

That is not baggage. That is clarity. And clarity is attractive.

## The standards *conversation*

I want to address something that comes up constantly when women over fifty talk about dating. The pressure to lower our standards because we are older.

No.

Your standards are not the problem. Your standards at fifty should be higher than they were at thirty, because you know more now. What you may need to adjust is not your standards but your expectations of the timeline. Good relationships at this stage of life take time to build. They tend to move more intentionally. That is not a problem. That is healthy.

You deserve someone who sees you. Who is interested in your mind and your life and your history. Who treats your time as valuable. Who shows up consistently, not just when it is convenient.

Give dating at fifty the chance it deserves. Give yourself that chance.
