There is a question that starts whispering sometime around your early fifties, and if you are not ready for it, it can feel like the floor is dropping out from under you.

Who are you when nobody needs you anymore?

Or maybe the question comes differently for you. Who are you now that the marriage is not what it was? Who are you outside of the job title you have held for twenty years? Who are you now that the children are grown and your whole identity as a mother has shifted into something quieter, less urgent, less constant?

This question — who am I, really, when I strip away all the roles — is the most important question you will grapple with in this chapter of your life. And it deserves more than a quick answer.

## The identity we build around *roles*

Psychologists have a concept called role identity theory, which says that much of how we understand ourselves is tied to the social roles we occupy. Mother. Wife. Manager. Caregiver. Daughter. When those roles change or end, the self-concept built around them can feel destabilized.

For Black women, this is complicated by additional layers. Many of us never had a strong cultural permission slip to build an identity outside of what we do for others. Our worth was tied to our productivity, our availability, our strength. The idea of sitting with the question of who we are for ourselves, separate from all of it, can feel indulgent.

It is not. It is essential.

## Starting to find *out*

What did you love before you became responsible for everyone else? Not what you are good at. Not what makes you useful. What did you genuinely love? Music, reading, drawing, dancing, cooking for the pleasure of it rather than the necessity? Whatever it was, it is still in you.

What have you always been curious about but never had time for? The thing you say someday about. That someday is now more available to you than it has ever been.

What makes you lose track of time? When are you so absorbed in something that you look up and an hour has passed without you noticing? That absorption is a clue.

> What would you do if nobody was watching and nobody was judging? That answer tells you something real about who you are underneath the performance.

## The identity that *emerges*

What I have watched happen in women who take this question seriously is remarkable. A woman who spent thirty years as a corporate administrator who discovers she is an artist. A woman who raised four children who finds out she is a community organizer. A woman who was defined entirely by her marriage who discovers she is a traveler, an adventurer, someone who wants to see the world and is perfectly capable of seeing it on her own terms.

These discoveries do not erase the previous chapters. They add to them. You are not becoming someone new. You are becoming more fully who you always were under the surface.

Fifty is not the age where your story ends. It is the age where you finally have the authority to write the next chapter yourself. You have the wisdom now that you did not have at thirty. You have fewer people to answer to.

Who are you? You are still finding out. And that is the most alive you can possibly be.
